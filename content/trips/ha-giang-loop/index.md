I almost skipped this trip. October is not the famous season, the forecast said fog, and my
backpack still smelled of the last rainy weekend. Then a friend sent a photo of buckwheat
fields turning pink and the decision made itself.

The first day is a warm-up. The road from the city climbs through Quản Bạ, and the twin
mountains wave you off as if you were leaving for good. By dinner my arms hurt from holding
the handlebars too tightly. Lesson one: let the bike lean and trust it.

On day two the road turns serious. Pine forest, thin air, a market where every stall sells
something I could not name. I ate thắng cố because a stranger insisted, and then ate it again
the next morning because it was, annoyingly, delicious.

Day three is the one everyone talks about. Mã Pì Lèng is a ribbon of road hanging over a
canyon so deep the river looks like a thread. The wind took my hat in the first minute and
I never saw it again. Somebody out there is wearing it with pride. We took the small boat on
the Nho Quế after lunch and floated through green water between walls of stone.

The last day is only distance. Eight hours, three coffee stops, and a sunset that made the
whole group go quiet for once.

**Good to know.** The loop is tiring rather than technical: long days, bad patches of road
and sudden fog. Ride with a partner, start early, and keep one day in reserve for weather.
